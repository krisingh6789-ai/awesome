Economy · Chapter 1

Indian Economy and Planning

भारतीय अर्थव्यवस्था और नियोजन

An economy allocates scarce resources among competing uses. India's development story is best understood through structural transformation, institutions and distribution: how workers move between sectors, how productive capacity grows, and who benefits. Planning is one method of coordinating development; it is not synonymous with public ownership of every activity.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • Growth is an increase in output; development also concerns capabilities, distribution, institutions and quality of life.
  • Primary, secondary and tertiary sectors classify activity, while organised/unorganised and public/private classifications answer different questions.
  • A high share of employment in a low-productivity sector can constrain average incomes even when another sector grows rapidly.
  • India's historical plans combined public investment, regulation and targets; NITI Aayog has a different advisory and cooperative role from the Planning Commission.
  • Market failures include externalities, public goods, asymmetric information and market power; government intervention also faces information and incentive problems.
  • Human capital, infrastructure, investment, technology and institutional reliability interact to influence long-run productivity.
  • For a policy proposal, identify the objective, instrument, implementing institution, financing and measurable outcome.

Recall & application

Exam trap: A larger GDP does not by itself prove lower inequality, improved health or adequate employment creation.

Original Mains prompt: Why is structural transformation necessary but insufficient for inclusive development in India?

Economy · Chapter 2

National Income Accounting

राष्ट्रीय आय लेखांकन

National accounts measure production, income and expenditure using a consistent boundary. Start with where production occurs, which period it belongs to, whether depreciation is deducted, and whether values are measured at current or constant prices. Many apparently difficult questions are classification problems rather than arithmetic problems.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • GDP measures the value of final production within domestic economic territory during a period; avoiding intermediate double counting is essential.
  • Gross value added equals output minus intermediate consumption. GDP at market prices equals aggregate GVA at basic prices plus product taxes minus product subsidies.
  • Gross national income equals GDP plus net primary income from the rest of the world; national and domestic are not interchangeable.
  • Net measures deduct consumption of fixed capital, commonly called depreciation in basic explanations.
  • The expenditure identity is GDP = C + I + G + (X − M); a rise in imports need not mean domestic welfare has fallen.
  • Nominal growth contains both price and volume changes. A price index or appropriate deflator is needed to compare real output.
  • GDP deflator = nominal GDP / real GDP × 100 when both use compatible valuation and coverage.
  • Unpaid household services, environmental depletion and distributional inequalities limit GDP as a welfare indicator.
  • Base years, methods and published series can change. Use the release date and methodology of MoSPI rather than assuming the photographed book's figures are current.

1. GDP measures production during a period

सकल घरेलू उत्पाद एक अवधि के उत्पादन का माप है, कुल संपत्ति का नहीं।

GDP is a flow: it measures the value of final goods and services produced within the domestic economy over a specified period. Wealth is a stock measured at a point in time. A country may have valuable land and buildings but low current output growth; another may increase current production while damaging natural assets. These are different dimensions of economic performance.

The word domestic directs attention to the location of production, not the nationality printed on a company's ownership documents. The word final helps prevent double counting of inputs already embodied in later output. Final use depends on the transaction: a household buying flour to consume and a bakery buying flour to make bread are using the same product differently.

Basis: IMF: Measuring GDP

2. Value added prevents the same output being counted repeatedly

हर चरण पर केवल नया जोड़ा गया मूल्य जोड़ें।

Imagine a deliberately simplified production chain with no other intermediate inputs. A farmer sells grain for ₹100, a mill sells flour for ₹160 and a bakery sells bread to households for ₹250. Adding every sale gives ₹510, but that counts the grain and flour again within the bread. Value added is ₹100 at the farm, ₹60 at the mill and ₹90 at the bakery: together ₹250.

The example assumes all production belongs to the same accounting period and ignores taxes, subsidies, inventory changes and other complications. Its purpose is to explain why intermediate consumption is deducted, not to reproduce a full national-accounts worksheet. If the bakery holds unsold output in inventory, it does not follow that the production simply disappears from accounting; the relevant inventory treatment must be considered.

Basis: IMF: production approach; original worked example

3. Three approaches describe the same production process

उत्पादन, आय और व्यय एक ही आर्थिक गतिविधि को अलग कोणों से देखते हैं।

The production approach adds value generated by producers. The income approach examines incomes generated in production, including compensation and operating surplus. The expenditure approach examines final purchases and relevant investment, government and external components. These are conceptually connected because production generates income and final output has a use.

Do not interpret the expenditure approach as adding every cash payment in the economy. A transfer payment is not itself the purchase of newly produced output. Purchasing an existing financial asset is different from producing a new machine. Imports must be handled so that spending on foreign production is not counted as domestic production. Actual statistical estimates can show discrepancies because data sources and measurement are imperfect.

Basis: IMF: production, expenditure and income approaches

4. Nominal growth and real growth answer different questions

कीमत बढ़ना और उत्पादन बढ़ना अलग-अलग हैं।

Nominal output is valued using current prices. Real measures attempt to separate changes in quantities from changes in prices. In a one-product example, output rises from 100 units at ₹10 to 110 units at ₹12. Nominal value rises from ₹1,000 to ₹1,320, or 32%. At the original price, the second period's output is ₹1,100, so real growth in this simplified example is 10%.

This is why a large increase in nominal GDP does not automatically mean an equally large improvement in actual production. In a many-product economy, aggregation and price measurement require statistical methods rather than one simple multiplication. India's current series, base year and official estimates must be taken from the applicable MoSPI release; this conceptual IMF source is not used to claim a current Indian base year.

Basis: IMF: Real GDP; original worked example

5. Gross versus net, and output versus well-being

घिसावट घटाने पर शुद्ध उत्पाद; जीवन की गुणवत्ता के लिए अतिरिक्त माप चाहिए।

Gross measures do not deduct consumption of fixed capital. Subtracting the relevant depreciation gives a net measure. Do not confuse this adjustment with subtracting imports, taxes or interest: each belongs to a different accounting question. A useful revision habit is to write beside every formula what conceptual boundary the adjustment changes.

GDP is valuable for tracking production, but a production total is not a complete account of distribution, unpaid care, environmental damage or quality of life. Even real output growth can coexist with weak employment opportunities for particular groups. A responsible answer therefore uses GDP for the question it measures and supplements it with appropriate employment, distributional, health, educational or environmental evidence for broader development questions.

Basis: IMF: Measuring GDP; Real GDP; original analytical application

IMF · Gross Domestic Product: An Economy’s All
Tim Callen, Finance & Development, Back to Basics; consulted 19 September 2026
GDP, value added, production/income/expenditure approaches and nominal versus real output. Used for concepts, not India's current base year or statistical methodology.

Work the accounts, not just the acronyms · earlier advanced draft

Consider a farmer selling wheat for 100, a mill selling flour for 160 and a baker selling bread to households for 250, with no other purchased inputs. The gross sales sum is 510, but the final product is worth 250. Value added is 100 + 60 + 90 = 250. This is why intermediate transactions cannot all be added again when estimating final production.

Now separate domestic production from residents’ primary incomes. GDP measures activity within the domestic economic territory. Add net primary income from the rest of the world to move to gross national income. Deduct consumption of fixed capital to move from gross to net. These are distinct adjustments: crossing a border and accounting for capital consumption answer different questions.

The expenditure identity classifies final uses. Household final consumption, investment including relevant inventory changes, government final consumption and net exports are not the same as every cash payment by households, firms or government. A pension is a transfer rather than payment for current production; a household’s subsequent purchase of a newly produced service may count as consumption.

For real growth, compare compatible constant-price volumes rather than nominal currency totals. If nominal GDP is 240 and real GDP is 200, the compatible deflator is 120. If the base year or methodology changes, splicing series without an official back-series or explanation can create false growth comparisons. Always record the series and release vintage.

Economy · Chapter 3

Inclusive Growth

समावेशी वृद्धि

Inclusive growth concerns participation in the growth process as well as the distribution of its benefits. It is broader than redistribution after growth has occurred: education, health, finance, infrastructure and fair access to markets affect who can produce, earn and influence decisions.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • Equal opportunity, decent work, social protection and access to basic services are connected dimensions.
  • Financial inclusion includes meaningful access, usage and quality—not merely an opened account.
  • Digital services can reduce transaction costs but connectivity, literacy and grievance redress determine whether exclusion persists.
  • A policy may raise average income while leaving regional, gender or social-group gaps unchanged.
  • Social spending should be assessed through outcomes and service quality as well as budget allocations.
  • Targeting can conserve resources but creates exclusion errors; universal provision has different fiscal and administrative trade-offs.
  • Disaggregate indicators and distinguish improvements in coverage from improvements in quality.

Recall & application

Exam trap: Growth that benefits some poor households is not automatically inclusive across all groups or regions.

Original Mains prompt: Design an inclusive-growth strategy that integrates jobs, capabilities and social protection.

Economy · Chapter 4

Unemployment and Poverty

बेरोजगारी और गरीबी

Labour statistics and poverty statistics describe different aspects of deprivation. A person can be employed and still be poor; a person outside the labour force is not automatically counted as unemployed. Always identify the survey definition, reference period and denominator before interpreting a headline number.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • The labour force includes employed persons and unemployed persons meeting the survey's seeking/availability criteria.
  • Unemployment rate = unemployed / labour force × 100; LFPR = labour force / relevant population × 100; WPR = employed / relevant population × 100.
  • Usual-status and current-weekly-status measures use different reference periods and cannot be compared casually.
  • Underemployment and disguised unemployment concern inadequate utilisation of labour, which a headline unemployment rate may miss.
  • Monetary poverty uses an income or consumption threshold; multidimensional poverty considers selected deprivations in several dimensions.
  • Poverty, inequality and vulnerability are related but distinct: a shock can impoverish a household not initially below a threshold.
  • Skill development alone cannot create demand for labour; investment, job quality, mobility and care responsibilities also matter.
  • Survey revisions and current poverty estimates require dated, comparable source data.

Recall & application

Exam trap: A fall in unemployment can accompany discouraged workers leaving the labour force; check LFPR and WPR before celebrating.

Original Mains prompt: Why should employment policy track job quality and labour-force participation alongside unemployment?

Part II · Banking & productive sectors

Economy · Chapter 5

Money and Banking

मुद्रा और बैंकिंग

Money supports exchange, accounting and storage of purchasing power. Banks connect payments, savings and credit, but bank lending is constrained by capital, liquidity, credit risk, regulation and demand. Monetary policy influences financial conditions through a chain of transmission rather than through a mechanical one-step command.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • The RBI is the central bank; commercial banks and other financial institutions perform different intermediary roles.
  • Currency held by the public and deposits appear in different monetary aggregates; an aggregate's definition matters before comparing growth rates.
  • A bank's deposit liability is not the same as its equity capital. Liquidity concerns timely payments; solvency concerns whether assets can cover liabilities.
  • Policy-rate changes can affect money-market rates, lending rates, demand and prices, but transmission has lags and varies by sector.
  • CRR concerns prescribed cash reserves with RBI; SLR concerns specified liquid assets held under the applicable framework.
  • Open-market purchases generally inject durable rupee liquidity; sales generally absorb it, other things equal.
  • Repo, standing facilities and the liquidity corridor must be studied through their actual operational definitions; rates and instruments can change.
  • Non-performing assets concern loan performance under applicable classification rules; a write-off is not automatically a waiver of a borrower's debt.
  • Inflation can arise from demand, costs, supply shocks and expectations; higher interest rates cannot directly create missing food supply.
  • Deposit insurance is limited and conditional under the applicable law; never assume every financial product has an unlimited guarantee.

Source-informed explanations

Source-informed original expansion · independent review pending · consulted 2026-09-19

1. Start with the policy problem, not a list of abbreviations

मौद्रिक नीति का मुख्य उद्देश्य मूल्य स्थिरता है, वृद्धि को ध्यान में रखते हुए।

Monetary policy influences financial conditions to support price stability while keeping growth in mind. Prices that rise rapidly and unpredictably make it harder for households to budget and businesses to plan. Yet suppressing demand too aggressively can also weaken output and employment. The policy problem involves uncertainty, lags and trade-offs rather than a mechanical promise to raise growth and reduce inflation immediately.

In flexible inflation targeting, a numerical target and tolerance range provide an anchor for expectations and accountability. The RBI overview consulted on 19 September 2026 reports a 4% CPI inflation target with a 2–6% tolerance band for April 2026 to March 2031. Treat this as a dated framework statement, not an eternal constitutional number. A target concerns the rate of inflation; it does not mean every product must rise by exactly 4%.

Basis: RBI: Monetary Policy Framework

2. The MPC decides the policy rate; implementation follows

नीतिगत दर का निर्णय और रोजमर्रा की तरलता व्यवस्था अलग चरण हैं।

The Monetary Policy Committee has six members under the statutory framework. The RBI overview describes at least four meetings each year, a quorum of four and a casting vote for the Governor in the event of a tie. These institutional rules help distinguish a committee decision from the idea that one official informally sets every interest rate in the economy.

The operating framework seeks to keep the weighted average call rate close to the policy repo rate. Actual implementation uses liquidity-management operations and market facilities. Commercial lending rates and deposit rates do not all change by an identical amount on the same day: funding structures, competition, borrower risk and contract terms affect transmission.

Basis: RBI: Monetary Policy Committee; Implementation of Monetary Policy

3. Repo, SDF and MSF: understand the corridor

रेपो के आसपास अल्पकालिक दरों को दिशा देने वाली व्यवस्था समझें।

Repo operations provide liquidity against eligible collateral under the relevant terms. The Standing Deposit Facility absorbs overnight liquidity without collateral and has served as the corridor floor since April 2022. The Marginal Standing Facility provides an overnight borrowing backstop and forms the upper side of the corridor. The policy repo rate sits between the floor and ceiling in the framework described by the RBI.

The corridor gives banks alternatives for placing surplus funds or obtaining short-term liquidity. That helps shape the incentives governing overnight market rates. A common outdated statement says the fixed reverse repo rate is always the current corridor floor. The RBI overview makes clear why a student must distinguish an older diagram from the framework after introduction of the SDF. No live policy-rate quotation is embedded here because rates can change.

Basis: RBI: Instruments of Monetary Policy

4. CRR and SLR are different requirements

नकद आरक्षित अनुपात और सांविधिक तरलता अनुपात को एक न समझें।

CRR concerns the prescribed cash balance that banks maintain with the RBI relative to the relevant demand and time liabilities. SLR concerns the prescribed holding of eligible liquid assets in India, such as the qualifying cash, gold and approved securities specified by the framework. The location, eligible assets and purpose of the requirements therefore differ.

An increase in a reserve requirement can affect how a bank allocates its balance sheet, but avoid claiming an exact one-for-one change in economy-wide lending without assumptions. Bank credit also depends on capital, borrower demand, creditworthiness, funding and risk appetite. The textbook reserve multiplier is a simplified model, not a guarantee that every rupee of reserves mechanically produces a fixed amount of actual lending.

Basis: RBI: Cash Reserve Ratio; Statutory Liquidity Ratio

5. Open-market operations are not the same as repo transactions

सरकारी प्रतिभूतियों की सीधी खरीद-बिक्री और अस्थायी लेनदेन अलग हैं।

An outright RBI purchase of government securities injects liquidity into the banking system, while an outright sale absorbs it, other things equal. Repo arrangements involve a different transaction structure and are used for liquidity provision on the applicable terms. Do not identify instruments only by whether government securities appear in the transaction; the legal and financial structure matters.

Original application: if banks face a temporary settlement-related liquidity shortage, a short-term operation addresses a different problem from a persistent system-wide liquidity deficit. Also distinguish banking-system liquidity from the solvency of an individual institution. Access to temporary funds cannot by itself repair losses that have eroded an institution's capital.

Basis: RBI: Open Market Operations; Repo operations

6. Trace transmission before predicting the result

दर बदलने से खर्च और कीमतों तक असर पहुँचने में समय लगता है।

A simplified tightening chain runs from a higher policy rate to tighter financial conditions, more expensive or less accessible borrowing, moderation in interest-sensitive spending and eventually reduced pressure on prices. Several links may be weak or delayed. Existing fixed-rate loans, abundant liquidity, bank balance-sheet conditions and expectations can change the speed of transmission.

A crop failure raises food prices through supply, not simply excessive borrowing. Monetary policy cannot directly create rainfall or new vegetables. It can still influence broader demand and the risk that a temporary shock feeds into persistent expectations and wider price increases. A Mains answer should distinguish the source of inflation and explain the role of supply-side and fiscal measures alongside monetary policy.

Basis: RBI: Monetary Policy Framework and implementation; original analytical application

7. Accountability is more than observing one monthly number

एक महीने की महँगाई और औपचारिक लक्ष्य-विफलता एक बात नहीं हैं।

Under the framework described on the RBI page, failure is assessed when average inflation lies outside the tolerance range for three consecutive quarters. The required report to government addresses reasons, proposed remedial action and the expected time to return inflation to target. A single monthly observation outside the band is therefore not identical to formal failure under that definition.

Policy statements, minutes and the Monetary Policy Report support transparency. Communication helps people understand why a decision was made, what risks were considered and how policy might respond to incoming evidence. For Prelims, distinguish the policy objective, decision-making body, operating target, instruments and accountability mechanism. For Mains, connect credibility to consistent explanation and realistic recognition of uncertainty.

Basis: RBI: inflation-target failure definition; transparency and communication

Follow the monetary transmission chain · earlier advanced draft

A central bank rate change first affects short-term financial conditions. Its influence on lending and deposit rates depends on funding structure, competition, benchmark links, balance-sheet health and expectations. Households and firms then adjust consumption and investment; changes in demand and expectations can influence inflation. Each arrow has lags and possible blockages.

Separate capital from reserves and liquidity. Equity capital absorbs losses. Reserve requirements concern prescribed assets or cash. A solvent institution can experience a liquidity run if short-term funding vanishes, while a highly liquid institution can still have serious underlying losses. Policy responses should match the problem rather than treating every banking difficulty as a shortage of cash.

A credit boom can conceal risk if repayment assessments rely on continuously rising collateral values. Once loans stop performing, banks may need provisioning, capital repair and credible resolution. An accounting write-off can clean a balance sheet presentation without giving the borrower a legal waiver. Recovery and restructuring therefore require their own analysis.

A food-supply shock illustrates the limits of monetary policy. Higher rates do not directly produce vegetables or repair transport, but may affect broader demand and second-round expectations. A balanced response distinguishes the initial supply constraint, fiscal or supply-side interventions, distributional consequences and inflation persistence. Current policy rates and targets must be obtained from the applicable dated RBI communication.

Economy · Chapter 6

Agriculture

कृषि

Agricultural outcomes depend on land, water, labour, technology, risk and market organisation. Productivity, farm income and food security overlap but are not identical objectives. A good answer follows the chain from inputs and production to storage, processing, sale and household welfare.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • Yield is output per unit of cultivated area; higher yield does not guarantee higher net income when input costs or prices change unfavourably.
  • Irrigation can reduce rainfall dependence but poorly priced extraction can deplete groundwater.
  • Small holdings can face disadvantages in marketing and access to equipment; aggregation and producer organisations may improve bargaining and services.
  • Minimum support prices, procurement, market prices and legal entitlement are distinct concepts; procurement is not universal across all crops and regions.
  • Food security has availability, access, utilisation and stability dimensions.
  • Storage, cold chains, logistics and processing can reduce losses and improve value realisation.
  • Credit and insurance help manage risk but depend on access, design, timely settlement and incentives.
  • Input subsidies, price support and direct transfers have different distributional and environmental effects.
  • Agricultural shares, MSP amounts and procurement rules must be dated and sourced.

Recall & application

Exam trap: Record food production can coexist with malnutrition if purchasing power, distribution, diet diversity or sanitation are inadequate.

Original Mains prompt: How can agricultural policy improve farmer incomes while protecting water, soil and food security?

Economy · Chapter 7

Industry

उद्योग

Industrial development expands productive capabilities through investment, learning and linkages. Assess manufacturing through employment, productivity, technology, scale and integration with suppliers rather than only its aggregate output share.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • Industrialisation can create demand for agriculture, logistics and services through backward and forward linkages.
  • Large firms may realise scale economies, while smaller firms can contribute employment and specialised supply relationships.
  • Formalisation can improve access to credit and protection but may impose transition costs on small enterprises.
  • Industrial policy can address coordination and learning failures but needs transparent selection, time limits and performance evaluation.
  • Credit constraints, electricity reliability, skills, land and logistics affect competitiveness.
  • The Index of Industrial Production measures selected output movements; it is not identical to the full national-accounts measure of industry.
  • Bankruptcy and exit arrangements can improve resource reallocation but must protect due process.
  • Current incentive schemes and enterprise-classification thresholds need updated sources.

Recall & application

Exam trap: An increase in output caused by prices is not necessarily an increase in physical production or productivity.

Original Mains prompt: What conditions are necessary for manufacturing growth to generate broad-based productive employment?

Economy · Chapter 8

Infrastructure

अवसंरचना

Infrastructure supplies network services that support production and human development. Projects often involve high upfront costs, long lives, externalities and coordination problems, making financing, regulation and maintenance central to policy.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • Transport, energy, water, sanitation, communications and social infrastructure have different service and funding models.
  • Network effects can raise the benefit of connecting an additional user when others are already connected.
  • Natural-monopoly characteristics may justify regulation of access, price or service quality.
  • A project can be financially weak but socially valuable; economic appraisal and private cash-flow appraisal answer different questions.
  • Construction completion is not the same as reliable service delivery; maintenance funding matters.
  • Land, environmental approvals, demand uncertainty and contract enforcement affect project risk.
  • Resilience and accessibility should be designed in, rather than treated solely as post-disaster repairs.

Part III · Public finance, investment & reform

Economy · Chapter 9

Government Budgeting

सरकारी बजट

A budget is both a financial plan and a statement of policy priorities. Read it through receipts, expenditure, balances, authorisation and outcomes. A deficit is a precisely defined accounting gap, not a synonym for every form of government debt or every macroeconomic problem.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • Revenue receipts include tax and non-tax receipts that do not create liabilities or reduce assets.
  • Capital receipts include borrowing and transactions such as recovery of loans or disinvestment; distinguish debt from non-debt receipts.
  • Revenue expenditure and capital expenditure are accounting categories; a revenue expense such as teacher salaries may still produce major social benefits.
  • Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts.
  • Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − revenue receipts − non-debt capital receipts. It indicates the financing gap met through borrowing and other liabilities under the accounting framework.
  • Primary deficit = fiscal deficit − interest payments.
  • Debt is a stock at a point in time; deficits are flows over a period. Valuation and other adjustments can affect the relationship.
  • An Appropriation Act authorises specified withdrawals, while taxation requires its own legal basis; a budget speech alone does not enact a tax.
  • Countercyclical fiscal policy can stabilise demand, but financing costs, inflation, external constraints and spending quality matter.
  • FRBM targets, escape provisions and current budget estimates require the applicable year's official documents.

Source-informed explanations

Source-informed original expansion · independent review pending · consulted 2026-09-19

1. Read a budget as a financing plan

बजट बताता है पैसा कहाँ से आएगा और कहाँ खर्च होगा।

A budget combines policy choices with accounting categories. Start with expenditure, then ask how it is financed through revenue receipts, non-debt capital receipts and borrowing. Borrowing is not ordinary income earned without a future obligation. Separating it from other receipts allows the fiscal deficit to show the financing gap rather than making the budget appear balanced simply because loans were counted as income.

Revenue receipts include tax and non-tax flows that do not have the same balance-sheet effect as borrowing or disposal of an asset. Non-debt capital receipts include items such as recovery of loans and other non-debt receipts. A loan recovery brings back an existing financial asset; it should not be casually described as a new tax. Read the exact budget classification instead of classifying every receipt as revenue.

Basis: Budget at a Glance 2026–27: opening explanation and receipts/expenditure table

2. Four deficits, four questions

राजकोषीय, राजस्व, प्रभावी राजस्व और प्राथमिक घाटा अलग प्रश्न पूछते हैं।

Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus receipts other than debt capital receipts. Revenue deficit equals revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts. Primary deficit equals fiscal deficit minus interest payments. Effective revenue deficit equals revenue deficit minus grants for creation of capital assets. Learn the reason for each subtraction, not just the abbreviation.

Primary deficit helps separate current borrowing needs from the interest burden on past debt. Effective revenue deficit recognises that certain grants are booked as revenue expenditure even though the recipient uses them to create capital assets. It does not mean all revenue expenditure is wasteful: teachers' salaries, maintenance and health services can be essential even when they do not appear as direct capital expenditure.

Basis: Budget at a Glance 2026–27: deficit definitions

3. Worked example from the 2026–27 Budget Estimates

ये 2026–27 के बजट अनुमान हैं, वास्तविक अंतिम खर्च नहीं।

The consulted table gives total expenditure of ₹53,47,315 crore, revenue receipts of ₹35,33,150 crore, recovery of loans of ₹38,397 crore and other non-debt receipts of ₹80,000 crore. Add the three non-debt receipt components: ₹35,33,150 + ₹38,397 + ₹80,000 = ₹36,51,547 crore. Subtract this from expenditure: ₹53,47,315 − ₹36,51,547 = ₹16,95,768 crore fiscal deficit.

The same Budget Estimates column gives interest payments of ₹14,03,972 crore. Therefore primary deficit = ₹16,95,768 − ₹14,03,972 = ₹2,91,796 crore. The published ratios are 4.3% of GDP for fiscal deficit and 0.7% for primary deficit. Those percentages are dated budget estimates and are rounded; do not use them as a timeless definition or as evidence of final realised accounts.

The arithmetic also reveals meaning. A substantial part of the financing gap is associated with interest on existing debt. Subtracting interest does not make that obligation disappear; it isolates a different analytical measure. Always write the unit and the estimate type when reproducing a budget figure.

4. BE, RE and actuals must not be mixed

बजट अनुमान, संशोधित अनुमान और वास्तविक आँकड़े अलग स्तंभ हैं।

Budget Estimates are the government's initial plan for the relevant financial year. Revised Estimates update expectations during the year. Actuals record the realised outcomes for the completed period represented in the accounts. In the consulted table, actuals for 2024–25, BE and RE for 2025–26 and BE for 2026–27 appear side by side.

A comparison can be meaningful only when its basis is stated. Growth from the previous year's BE can differ from growth over the previous year's RE, especially if spending fell short of the original allocation. Original application: if a programme had BE 100, RE 80 and next-year BE 100, the new allocation is unchanged relative to the old plan but 25% above the revised estimate. Neither percentage should be presented without its comparison base.

Basis: Budget at a Glance 2026–27: table column headings

5. Capital expenditure is important, but classification is not a verdict

पूँजीगत वर्गीकरण और खर्च की गुणवत्ता एक ही बात नहीं हैं।

Effective capital expenditure adds capital expenditure and grants for creation of capital assets. This addresses the distinction between the Union's accounting classification and assets created by recipients of its grants. It helps explain why looking at a single expenditure line can miss part of the investment effort.

Still, an asset can be badly chosen, delayed or poorly maintained. Meanwhile, some revenue spending makes existing assets useful: a hospital building needs staff, medicines, electricity and maintenance. A good answer evaluates project selection, completion, complementary services and outcomes. 'Capital good, revenue bad' is not an adequate public-finance analysis.

Basis: Budget at a Glance 2026–27: effective capital expenditure definition; original application

6. Evaluate a deficit through context and sustainability

घाटे का आकार, कारण, उपयोग और वित्तपोषण साथ देखें।

A deficit is an accounting measure; its economic significance depends on circumstances. Borrowing for productive capacity during weak demand can have different consequences from persistent borrowing that finances ineffective expenditure when inflation pressures are already high. The financing cost, maturity structure, growth prospects and credibility of future policy also matter.

Do not confuse a deficit, which is a flow over a period, with public debt, which is a stock at a point in time. Nor should a primary balance be interpreted as the disappearance of debt or interest. For Mains, separate the immediate stabilisation role of fiscal policy from the medium-term need to manage obligations and improve the quality of public spending.

Basis: Budget definitions; original economic application rather than a claim from the budget table

Read a budget using identities and trade-offs · earlier advanced draft

Suppose revenue receipts are 700, revenue expenditure 780, capital expenditure 220 and non-debt capital receipts 50. Total expenditure is 1,000. Revenue deficit is 80; fiscal deficit is 250. If interest payments are 90, primary deficit is 160. The figures are a teaching example, not India’s actual budget data.

The primary balance removes interest from the current deficit calculation, but interest obligations are still real payments. A country with a primary surplus may still have an overall deficit if interest payments are large. Debt dynamics depend on primary balances, growth, effective interest rates and valuation or other adjustments, so a single deficit percentage cannot answer every sustainability question.

Spending quality needs more than accounting labels. Teacher salaries are normally revenue expenditure but can support human capital. An unused building may be recorded as capital expenditure yet yield little benefit. Examine selection, procurement, completion, maintenance and outcomes before equating capital spending with efficiency or revenue spending with waste.

Countercyclical policy can support demand in a downturn, but implementation timing, leakages, spare capacity and financing matter. Long-gestation projects approved too late may deliver stimulus after conditions have changed. Transparent medium-term plans can preserve useful investment while making trade-offs and fiscal risks visible.

Investment Models

निवेश मॉडल

Investment models assign ownership, financing, construction, operation and risk among public and private participants. A label such as PPP does not establish value for money; the contract and actual allocation of controllable risks determine outcomes.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • Appraisal should compare lifecycle costs, service standards, affordability and realistic demand forecasts.

Part IV · Trade & international institutions

Economy · Chapter 12

International Economics

अंतरराष्ट्रीय अर्थशास्त्र

An open economy exchanges goods, services, income and financial claims with the rest of the world. Separate the flow of trade from the financing of external balances, and distinguish currency prices from domestic purchasing power.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • Comparative advantage depends on relative opportunity cost, not merely absolute productivity.
  • The current account includes goods, services, primary income and secondary income; the trade balance commonly refers to goods alone.
  • Capital and financial-account terminology differs between introductory Indian usage and international statistical presentation; check the framework before classifying a flow.
  • FDI generally involves a lasting interest and influence; portfolio investment concerns tradable financial claims without the same control relationship.
  • Currency depreciation means a fall in value under market movements; devaluation refers to an official adjustment in a fixed or managed parity context.
  • Depreciation may aid export competitiveness but can raise import costs, inflation and foreign-currency debt burdens.
  • External sustainability depends on maturity, currency, composition and use of financing, not only the size of a current-account deficit.
  • Foreign-exchange reserves are assets of the monetary authority, not simply an unrestricted government spending balance.

Source-informed explanations

Source-informed original expansion · independent review pending · consulted 2026-09-19

1. MFN is a rule against arbitrary discrimination among partners

सर्वाधिक अनुकूल राष्ट्र सामान्यतः समान व्यापारिक व्यवहार का नियम है।

Most-favoured-nation treatment generally requires that a trade advantage offered to one member be extended to other members in the relevant agreement's setting. The name can mislead: it is not simply a special friendship label reserved for a single favourite country. Its purpose is to limit discrimination among trading partners.

The rule has conditional exceptions, including qualifying regional trade agreements and certain preferences for developing countries. Trade-remedy measures also operate under their own legal conditions. Therefore both 'MFN forbids every exception' and 'a government can discriminate whenever it wishes' are misleading. The actual agreement and exception must be identified.

Basis: WTO: Trade without discrimination; Most-favoured-nation treatment

2. National treatment asks what happens after market entry

राष्ट्रीय व्यवहार का प्रश्न घरेलू बाजार में आयातित और स्थानीय वस्तुओं के व्यवहार से जुड़ता है।

National treatment concerns the comparison between foreign and domestic products or covered interests once the relevant market-entry stage has been crossed. For goods, the central idea is that internal taxes and regulation should not be manipulated to undermine the treatment required by the agreement. This is different from MFN, which compares treatment among foreign trading partners.

An import duty at the border does not, by itself, violate national treatment merely because an identical duty is not imposed on domestic goods. The tariff must instead be assessed under the relevant tariff commitments and other rules. Original example: distinguish a customs tariff on imported shoes from an internal tax designed to burden imported shoes more heavily after import. The two measures raise different legal questions.

Basis: WTO: National treatment

3. Bound and applied tariffs are not synonyms

बद्ध शुल्क सीमा और वास्तव में लगाया गया शुल्क अलग हो सकते हैं।

A bound tariff is a committed ceiling in the relevant schedule. An applied tariff is the rate actually used. A country may apply a rate below its bound level, leaving a difference between current policy and its ceiling. The existence of a binding therefore does not automatically mean the applied tariff is zero.

Original numerical example: assume a product has a bound rate of 40% and an applied rate of 15%. Those figures describe different things. Whether and how a change is permitted requires considering the commitment and other applicable obligations, not merely declaring that all tariff increases breach WTO rules. Exceeding a binding raises a different problem from changing an applied rate within the binding.

Basis: WTO: Predictability through binding and transparency

4. Predictability can matter as much as today's tariff

व्यापार में स्थिर और पारदर्शी नियम निवेश के निर्णयों में मदद करते हैं।

A producer deciding whether to invest in an export facility cares about the reliability of market access, not only the tariff today. Published rules, notification requirements and bindings can reduce uncertainty. Sudden opaque changes may discourage investment even when a market briefly offers a low tariff.

This helps explain why WTO principles are not adequately described as simply 'free trade'. The system permits tariffs in many circumstances while seeking commitments, transparency, progressive liberalisation and rules against specified discriminatory or unfair practices. Predictability is a distinct institutional benefit, not proof that every trade barrier has been eliminated.

Basis: WTO: Predictability; transparency

5. Goods, services and intellectual property have different rules

GATT, GATS और TRIPS को एक ही समझौता न मानें।

GATT concerns trade in goods, GATS concerns services and TRIPS concerns intellectual property. Familiar ideas such as non-discrimination appear across the system, but their legal operation and exceptions differ. A principle stated for goods should not automatically be transplanted into services without examining the agreement and commitments.

For services, sectoral commitments and the relevant mode of supply matter. For intellectual property, the issue may concern protection and treatment rather than a tariff on a physical product. A strong Prelims approach first identifies the agreement and subject; a strong Mains approach explains why differing sectors require differing commitments instead of treating WTO law as one universal zero-barrier rule.

Basis: WTO: principles discussion of GATT, GATS and TRIPS

6. Development flexibilities and domestic adjustment

व्यापार खुलने के लाभ और समायोजन की लागत का वितरण देखें।

The WTO overview recognises that developing economies may need flexibility and time in implementing commitments. These provisions must be read as specific legal arrangements, not as a blanket exemption from every obligation. Likewise, participation in a rules-based trading system does not guarantee that every region or worker gains equally from every change.

For an original Mains application, evaluate a tariff reduction through several channels: consumer prices, imported input costs, competition for domestic firms, export opportunities and the capacity of workers and firms to adjust. Complementary skills, infrastructure and social protection can affect the outcome. Do not claim that protection always creates efficiency or that immediate liberalisation has no adjustment costs.

Basis: WTO: encouraging development and economic reform; original analytical application

Separate trade flows, financing and currency risk · earlier advanced draft

Start with the current account: goods, services, primary income and secondary income. A merchandise deficit can coexist with large service exports and transfers, so the current-account balance need not equal the merchandise balance. Use a consistent sign convention before combining components.

External financing differs in maturity and risk. Stable equity participation in a productive project differs from short-term foreign-currency borrowing used to finance an asset with domestic-currency revenues. Both may bring resources, but repayment obligations, control rights and vulnerability to sudden reversals differ.

Currency depreciation changes relative prices but does not guarantee an immediate export boom. Exporters may need imported inputs, production capacity may be constrained, contracts may adjust slowly and foreign demand may be weak. Meanwhile, foreign-currency debt and essential imports can become more expensive in domestic currency.

A reserve buffer can support confidence and liquidity, but reserves are not costless or a substitute for sustainable financing. Assessment should consider import needs, short-term debt, currency mismatches, maturity concentration and access to stable financing, rather than treating one reserve number as sufficient.

Part VI · Glossary & source resources

Economy · Chapter 17

Glossary

अर्थशास्त्र शब्दावली

Use the glossary to make distinctions precise before solving questions. A definition is most useful when paired with a contrast and a simple example; avoid memorising a term without its denominator, time dimension or institutional context.

Core explanation · earlier draft, review pending

  • Stock: measured at a point in time, such as public debt. Flow: measured over an interval, such as an annual deficit.
  • Inflation: sustained increase in a general price level. Disinflation: a slower positive inflation rate. Deflation: a fall in the general price level.
  • Liquidity: capacity to meet payments when due. Solvency: ability to meet liabilities in an overall balance-sheet sense.
  • Opportunity cost: value of the best forgone alternative. A sunk cost has already been incurred and cannot be recovered by the current choice.
  • Externality: an effect on a third party not fully reflected in the market transaction. A public good is non-rival and non-excludable in the standard definition.
  • Real variables adjust for prices; nominal variables are measured at current monetary values.
  • Progressive taxation imposes a rising average tax burden as the relevant base rises; statutory labels alone do not settle actual incidence.
  • Crowding out concerns displacement of private activity through financing or resource constraints; crowding in can occur when public investment improves private returns.
  • Elasticity measures proportional responsiveness; a slope measures change in original units. They are not interchangeable.
  • Moral hazard concerns incentives after an arrangement; adverse selection concerns hidden information before selection.

Recall & application

Exam trap: The same word can have a narrower statistical definition than its everyday usage. Use the definition relevant to the question.
